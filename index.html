PART 2 • ADJUSTMENTS & OTHER INCOME SOURCES

2. Adjustments & Other Common Incomes

Form 1099-INT
! Verify with Form ⓘ
Taxable Interest
$
Bank savings interest reported on 1099-INT.
💡 Taxable Interest Income
What does this mean?

Interest earned from bank high-yield savings accounts, CDs, checking accounts, and peer lending.

Where to find it:

Form 1099-INT Box 1 sent in January by your financial institutions, or via online bank tax documents.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Banks send Copy A directly to the IRS. If you omit over $10 of interest, the IRS automated matching system flags it.

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

Check your online banking portal in early February. Download your 1099-INT and enter the total interest.

Schedule D / Form 8949
! Must Report If Sold ⓘ
Net Capital Gains & Losses
$
$
Stocks, crypto, or property sales from 1099-B. Losses offset income up to -$3,000 cap.
💡 Capital Gains & Losses (Schedule D)
What does this mean?

Profits or losses from selling capital assets like stocks, mutual funds, ETFs, cryptocurrency, or real estate.

Where to find it:

Form 1099-B provided by your brokerages (Robinhood, Fidelity, Charles Schwab, Coinbase, etc.).

Why it matters for your taxes:

Brokerages report proceeds to the IRS. If you don't report sales, the IRS may assume your cost basis was $0! If you had net losses, you can deduct up to $3,000 ($1,500 MFS) against ordinary wages.

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

If you sold no investments outside tax-sheltered accounts (401k/IRA), leave at $0. If you suffered losses, claiming the -$3,000 deduction saves you hundreds of dollars in taxes.

Schedule C Part II
! Money Saver ⓘ
Freelance Business Expenses
$
Ordinary & necessary business expenses reducing your 1099-NEC earnings.
💡 Schedule C Business Expenses
What does this mean?

Costs you incurred to earn self-employment / 1099 income (mileage, software, laptop, home office, supplies).

Where to find it:

Your receipts, bank statements, mileage logs, and bookkeeping software for independent contractor activity.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Business expenses directly reduce your Schedule C net profit dollar-for-dollar, cutting both your income tax and 15.3% self-employment tax!

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

Never pay taxes on gross 1099 revenue! Deduct all legitimate business expenses you have receipts or records for.

Schedule 1 Line 21
! Money Saver ⓘ
Student Loan Interest
$
Deductible interest paid on student loans (up to $2,500).
💡 Student Loan Interest Deduction
What does this mean?

Above-the-line deduction for interest paid during the year on eligible higher education student loans.

Where to find it:

Form 1098-E Box 1 from your loan servicer (Aidvantage, MOHELA, Nelnet, Sallie Mae).

Why it matters for your taxes:

Capped at $2,500 per year. It directly reduces your AGI, lowering your tax bill without requiring itemizing.

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

If you paid at least $600 in student loan interest, your servicer is required to send you Form 1098-E. Always enter it!

Schedule 1 Line 20
! Money Saver ⓘ
Traditional IRA Deduction
$
Pre-tax contributions made to traditional IRAs.
💡 Traditional IRA Contribution Deduction
What does this mean?

Deductible contributions made directly to an individual retirement arrangement (IRA) up to annual limits ($7,000 / $8,000 if 50+).

Where to find it:

Your personal IRA account transaction history or Form 5498.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Reduces your taxable income dollar-for-dollar. You can make contributions for the tax year until Tax Day (mid-April)!

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

Roth IRA contributions are NOT deductible (they grow tax-free later). Only enter Traditional IRA contributions here.

Form 8889 / Sched 1 Line 13
! Money Saver ⓘ
HSA Deduction (After-Tax Contributions)
$
Personal after-tax contributions made to a qualified HSA ($4,150 single / $8,300 family).
💡 Health Savings Account (HSA Form 8889)
What does this mean?

Contributions you made directly from your checking account into an HSA (not via payroll pre-tax paycheck deductions).

Where to find it:

Form 5498-SA or your HSA custodian contribution summary for the tax year.

Why it matters for your taxes:

This is an above-the-line deduction that lowers your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) even if you claim the standard deduction.

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

Do NOT enter contributions that were already made through employer payroll (shown with code W in Box 12 of your W-2), as those were already excluded from Box 1 wages!

Tax Credits
! ! Money Saver ⓘ
Qualifying Children under 17
$2,000 Child Tax Credit per eligible child.
💡 Child Tax Credit (Qualifying Children)
What does this mean?

Children under age 17 at year-end who lived with you for more than half the year and whom you supported.

Where to find it:

Your dependents' Social Security cards and birth dates.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Worth up to $2,000 per child directly subtracted from your federal tax liability, with up to $1,700 refundable (Additional Child Tax Credit).

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

One of the largest refund drivers on US tax returns. Make sure the child has a valid SSN issued before the return due date.

PART 3 • SCHEDULE A ITEMIZED DEDUCTIONS VS. STANDARD DEDUCTION

3. Schedule A Itemized Deductions vs. Standard Deduction

The IRS allows you to claim whichever reduces your taxes more: your fixed Standard Deduction or the itemized sum of your eligible personal expenses.
Schedule A Line 1
>7.5% AGI Only ⓘ
Medical & Dental Expenses
$
Out-of-pocket medical costs exceeding 7.5% of your AGI are deductible.
💡 Medical and Dental Expenses (Schedule A)
What does this mean?

Total unreimbursed out-of-pocket doctor visits, prescriptions, surgeries, therapy, dental and vision bills.

Where to find it:

Health insurance Explanations of Benefits (EOBs), pharmacy receipts, medical bills.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Strict 7.5% AGI floor: only medical expenses IN EXCESS of 7.5% of your AGI count toward itemizing.

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

For most filers without catastrophic medical costs, the 7.5% floor makes this $0. If you had major surgery, add it up!

Schedule A Line 5b
! $10k Cap ⓘ
Real Estate / Property Taxes
$
County/city property taxes on home/land. Combined with state tax withheld (max $10k cap).
💡 State and Local Taxes (SALT Cap)
What does this mean?

Real estate property taxes you paid on your primary residence, vacation home, or personal property.

Where to find it:

Form 1098 Box 10 (from mortgage servicer) or county tax assessor bills.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Federal law caps the total SALT deduction (sum of state income taxes + property taxes) at $10,000 ($5,000 if married filing separately).

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

The engine automatically combines your W-2 state withholding (Box 17) with this property tax number and caps it at $10,000.

Schedule A Line 8a
! High Savings ⓘ
Home Mortgage Interest
$
Interest paid on home loans up to $750,000 principal reported on Form 1098.
💡 Home Mortgage Interest (Form 1098)
What does this mean?

Interest paid on acquisition indebtedness used to buy, build, or substantially improve your primary or second home.

Where to find it:

Form 1098 Box 1 sent in January by your mortgage lender (Chase, Wells Fargo, Rocket, etc.).

Why it matters for your taxes:

This is usually the #1 expense that pushes homeowners past the Standard Deduction threshold!

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

If you bought a home recently with mortgage rates at 6-7%, your interest often easily exceeds the Standard Deduction.

Schedule A Line 11
Money Saver ⓘ
Gifts to Charity (Cash & Goods)
$
Donations to 501(c)(3) churches, non-profits, and educational institutions.
💡 Charitable Contributions (Schedule A)
What does this mean?

Donations of cash, check, or property to qualifying non-profit 501(c)(3) organizations.

Where to find it:

Year-end contribution receipts or written acknowledgments from the charities.

Why it matters for your taxes:

Reduces your taxable income when itemizing. For donations >$250, you must keep a written receipt from the charity.

📌 Pro-Tip / Verdict:

Political contributions or GoFundMe personal fundraisers are NOT tax deductible.
